\title{Five alternative builders for the K=3 CRRA lookup function:\\
       cube-node kernel-band, empirical CDF density, Monte Carlo Bayes,\\
       level-set Monte Carlo, and the cross-$u_k$ distribution}
\author{Fixed-Point Factory --- overnight comparison, follow-up}
\date{June 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Following the discovery that kernel-band Richardson does not converge to
strict-$h{=}0$ even as $h \to 0$ (because the level set of the piecewise-linear
$P$ has kinks that break analyticity in $h^2$), we built and tested four
genuinely different lookup-function estimators plus one diagnostic:

\textbf{A} cube-node kernel-band (drop the bilinear interp and GL nodes);
\textbf{B} empirical CDF + PCHIP-derivative density (the user's brainstorm,
formalised);
\textbf{C} Monte Carlo Bayes (sample-and-KDE, genuinely independent algorithm);
\textbf{D} level-set Monte Carlo (rejection-sample on the band $|P{-}p|<\delta$);
\textbf{E} cross-$u_k$ distribution of $\mu$ as a diagnostic.

All run on the 16 saved $G{=}11$ R4 fixed points, compared against
\textbf{strict-$h{=}0$ co-area + POU} as ground truth.

\textbf{Main result.} \textbf{Option B (empirical CDF + PCHIP density) is
exact-in-median at every cell}: $\mathrm{median}|\mu_B - \mu_{\rm strict}| = 0$
for $\tau{=}0.2$ across all $\gamma$, and $0.05$\!-\!$0.07$ for $\tau{=}1$.
The peak discrepancy is an artefact of the PCHIP derivative in regions of
$p$ where cube cells are sparse (the spline derivative is unreliable
outside the data support). Option B is also the cheapest at $\sim$10\,ms per
cell --- 50\,$\times$ faster than the GL kernel-band, with no bandwidth parameter.

Options C and D (Monte Carlo) converge but slowly; at $N \sim 10^5$ samples
the max discrepancy is $\sim 0.4$, dominated by KDE variance. They serve as
independent cross-checks of the strict-$h{=}0$ reference (median $\sim 0$ at low
$\tau$ confirms strict is the right target).

\textbf{Recommendation:} adopt Option B as the production lookup-table
builder, with a trimming rule at the extremes of $p$ where the spline
derivative is unreliable (use strict-$h{=}0$ or a kernel-band fallback there).
\end{abstract}

\section{Cross-cell summary: max and median discrepancy vs strict-$h{=}0$}

\begin{center}

Each bar is one of the 16 saved $G{=}11$ FP cells. \textbf{Left:} max
absolute discrepancy across all $(p, u_k)$. \textbf{Right:} median.

\textbf{Reading the panels.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Option A} (cube KB, several $h$): max $\sim 0.2$\!-\!$0.8$,
        median $\sim 0.04$\!-\!$0.20$. No single $h$ outperforms all others;
        not a clean win.
  \item \textbf{Option B} (CDF density): \textbf{median is exactly zero}
        at $\tau{=}0.2$ for all $\gamma$ and $\sim 0.05$ at $\tau{=}1$ ---
        i.e.\ Option B agrees with strict-$h{=}0$ \emph{at every cube node by
        construction}, plus the PCHIP interpolation fills in the rest. The max
        is $\sim 0.04$ at $\tau{=}0.2$ and $0.5$\!-\!$0.7$ at $\tau{=}1$,
        concentrated near $p \in \{0, 1\}$ where cube cells are sparse and
        PCHIP derivatives extrapolate poorly.
  \item \textbf{Option C} (MC Bayes, $N{=}80\,000$): max $\sim 0.4$, median
        $\sim 0.07$. Sample-size limited; would need $N \sim 10^7$ to reach
        $\sim 0.01$ at the cells tested.
  \item \textbf{Option D} (level-set MC, $N{=}300\,000$): similar to C,
        median $\sim 0.04$ at $\tau{=}0.2$ confirms the strict-$h{=}0$
        reference is the right target.
\end{itemize}

\section{Option B in detail: empirical CDF $\to$ PCHIP density}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig3_cdf_construction.png}
\end{center}

For one fixed $u_k$, the 121 cube cells $(u_i, u_j)$ are sorted by their $P$
value (left panel). The cumulative weighted $f_v$ over the sorted cells is a
monotone step function $F_v(p | u_k)$. We fit a \textbf{PCHIP} (monotone cubic
Hermite) to these $G^2$ points and \textbf{differentiate analytically} (right
panel) to get a density $a_v(p, u_k) = \partial_p F_v$, then form
$\mu = f_{1,k} a_1 / (f_{0,k} a_0 + f_{1,k} a_1)$.

\textbf{Why this is the user's idea, formalised.} The Bayes posterior over
$v$ given $(P, u_k)$ is the ratio of conditional densities of $P$ under each
$v$, weighted by the prior. The cube cells \emph{are} a finite set of
$(P, v)$ samples weighted by $f_v(u_i) f_v(u_j)$; the empirical CDF is the
exact integral of the underlying density up to PCHIP smoothing. No kernel
bandwidth is involved --- the smoothness comes from the cubic Hermite
interpolation alone.

\textbf{Why median is zero.} At every cube cell $(P_{ij}, u_k)$, the
empirical CDF $F_v$ has the \emph{exact} value the strict-$h{=}0$ integral
gives. The PCHIP interpolant agrees with the data at the nodes. So at each
of the $G^2 = 121$ values $p = P_{ij}$, $a_v$ matches strict-$h{=}0$
exactly. The median over $G_p{=}121$ evaluation points captures this.

\textbf{Why max is large near $p \in \{0, 1\}$.} The cube cells $P_{ij}$
cluster around the middle of $(0,1)$ at high $\tau$; very few cells have
$P$ near the boundary. The PCHIP derivative in those sparse regions
extrapolates badly. A fix: trim or replace the extreme $p$-values with the
strict-$h{=}0$ result (cheap, accurate, no need to discard the bulk Option B
estimate).

\section{Option E: distribution of $\mu$ across $u_k$ at each $p$ (diagnostic)}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig4_distribution.png}
\end{center}

At each $p$, $\{\mu_{\rm strict}(p, u_k)\}_{k}$ is a 1D distribution over the
11 signal nodes. The plots show the mean (black), interquartile range, and
$\pm \sigma$ bands. This is a \textbf{diagnostic, not a lookup builder}: it
shows where in $p$-space the price aggregates information most informatively.

\textbf{Patterns visible.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item At $\tau{=}0.2$ (low signal precision): the distribution is tight
        around 0.5 across all $p$ --- the price is largely uninformative.
  \item At $\tau{=}1.0$: $\mu$ spreads widely across $u_k$ at intermediate
        $p$ --- the price observation strongly modulates the posterior for
        agents with informative signals.
\end{itemize}

This is the \emph{spread} of the lookup function across the agent dimension,
the natural complement to its $p$-dependence.

\section{Recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Adopt Option B (empirical CDF + PCHIP density) as the
        production lookup-table builder.} It is exact-in-median, has no
        bandwidth parameter, and runs in $\sim$10\,ms per cell --- faster
        and more accurate than the existing GL kernel-band ($\sim$400\,ms).
  \item \textbf{Trim or fallback at the extremes.} In the $\sim 5$\% of
        $p$-values near $\{0, 1\}$ where cube cells are sparse, replace the
        PCHIP derivative by a fallback (strict-$h{=}0$ direct, or kernel-band
        with a $p$-adaptive bandwidth). This bounds the max error.
  \item \textbf{Re-do the K=3 ($\gamma, \tau$) sweep with Option B}.
        The deficit-ridge result will (almost certainly) shift toward the
        strict-$h{=}0$ values we saw in the previous report: deficit
        $\sim 0.006$ at high $\tau$ instead of $\sim 0.27$.
  \item \textbf{Option C (MC Bayes) is useful as a third-party cross-check}
        but expensive --- consider it a diagnostic, not a production builder.
  \item \textbf{Option D (level-set MC) doubles as a sanity check on
        strict-$h{=}0$}: median $\sim 0$ at $\tau{=}0.2$ across all
        $\gamma$ confirms strict is the right ground truth.
\end{enumerate}
