\section*{What this is}

A 1000-cell ladder of $\tau$ from 0.001 to 1.0 in steps of 0.001, at
$\gamma{=}100$, $G{=}7$, solved with the \textbf{Lin-CDF R4 kernel-band
operator in double-double arithmetic}. Newton steps use an
\textbf{analytic Jacobian} (chain rule through the bilinear interp +
Gaussian kernel-band + Richardson combine + linear-interp lookup + CRRA
implicit derivative). Per cell: $\sim$1--1.3\,s.

\textbf{Important caveat.} R4 kernel-band does NOT extrapolate to the
strict-$h{=}0$ limit (we documented this earlier: bias $\sim 0.5$ in $\mu$,
$\sim 0.36$ in $P$ at high $\tau$). These are R4 fixed points at high
precision, not the "true" strict-$h{=}0$ equilibria.

\section{F floor along the ladder}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig1_F.png}
\end{center}

Median $|F|_\infty \sim 10^{-18}$, dispersed $10^{-26}$ to $10^{-16}$.
\emph{Not} full DD eps ($10^{-25}$ target) because the analytic Jacobian
is in float64 (accumulated rounding $\sim 10^{-13}$ from $\sim 31\,000$
inner additions per entry). To reach DD eps we'd need a DD-precision
Jacobian (substantial port).

\section{Slope and deficit along the ladder}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig2_slope_deficit.png}
\end{center}

\textbf{Slope $\alpha^*$}: monotone increasing in $\tau$ from $\sim 0.07$
at $\tau{=}0.001$ to higher values; smooth across the ladder.

\textbf{Deficit $1-R^2_\mathrm{nonparam}$}: rapidly grows from
near-zero to substantial values as $\tau$ increases past 0.05--0.1
(kicks in around $\tau \sim 0.1$ where the deficit-ridge regime starts).
\emph{The deficit values here are inflated by the kernel-band bias} ---
strict-$h{=}0$ deficits at the same $(\gamma, \tau)$ would be 5--40$\times$
smaller per our earlier cross-validation.

\section{Monotonicity along the ladder}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig3_monotonicity.png}
\end{center}

\textbf{Number of monotonicity violations} ($\partial P/\partial u_i < 0$)
grows from zero at very low $\tau$ to dozens past $\tau \sim 0.1$. The
\textbf{worst negative slope} reaches $\sim -0.01$ at moderate $\tau$.

The non-monotonicity is the structural signal: the R4 operator's FP is
genuinely non-monotone in the high-$\tau$ regime. We documented earlier
that the "true" Bayesian equilibrium SHOULD be monotone -- so these R4
FPs differ from the underlying equilibrium in this respect.

\section{Honest summary}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ladder is fast}: $\sim$1.2\,s/cell with analytic Jacobian,
        ~18\,min total for 1000 cells.
  \item \textbf{F floors at $\sim 10^{-18}$}: float64 Jacobian limits
        Newton precision; not full DD eps.
  \item \textbf{R4 FPs are biased}: relative to strict-$h{=}0$, by
        $\sim 0.5$ in $\mu$ at high $\tau$. Deficit values inflated.
  \item \textbf{Monotonicity violations}: the R4 FPs are non-monotone at
        high $\tau$ (up to 30 cells violate, worst $\sim -0.01$).
  \item \textbf{Useful for}: tracking R4 behavior across $\tau$, warm-start
        for other operators, diagnostic.
  \item \textbf{Not useful for}: claiming strict-$h{=}0$ equilibria.
\end{itemize}
