\section*{The anchor}
\texttt{projects/REZN/solved\_fixed\_points/k3\_coarea\_limit/} contains
six nailed fixed points along the joint central-path
$h = C \sqrt{\Delta u}$ ($C = 0.45$): residual $F_\infty$ from
$6 \cdot 10^{-15}$ at $G = 9$ to $\sim 10^{-11}$ at $G = 31$, with the
one-to-one revelation deficit converging to $0.282\!-\!0.291$. This
\emph{is} the reference value of the K=3 deterministic equilibrium at
this $(\tau, \gamma)$ --- the only object in the repo I trust to four
digits. The cross-check here tests other methods against it on the
\emph{same} cell.

\section*{The headline}
\textbf{All three production-side methods miss the anchor deficit by
\textit{a quarter of the unit interval}.} Self-residual at the
arithmetic limit (M1, M2 at $10^{-27}\!-\!10^{-29}$) is no protection.
Even the bare \mbox{strict-$h{=}0$} best-iterate (M3) misses by the
same amount.

\section*{Reading}
\begin{itemize}
  \item M1 / M2 are R4 Newton-nailed fixed points of their respective
        operators. Their deficits are near zero because at $h$ on the
        order of the cube cell spacing, the kernel-band Phi is
        Lipschitz, the equilibrium is essentially \emph{fully revealing}
        for that operator, and DD just pins that wrong limit to 29
        digits.
  \item M3 reaches a strict best-iterate with $F = 0.088$ --- the
        \mbox{strict-$h{=}0$} stall, documented in Findings 9 and 17 of
        the monograph. Its deficit is also near zero for the same
        reason: the best iterate found by damped Picard at $G = 9$ is
        near the FR branch.
  \item The anchor's deficit comes from the \emph{joint} limit
        $h\downarrow 0$ with $G\uparrow$ at fixed ratio. Neither
        fixed-$h$ Newton nor strict-Picard alone follows that
        central path.
  \item This is the same lesson as the $h$-ladder at $(\tau = 0.1,
        \gamma = 10)$ from earlier: the legacy $h$ values bias the
        slope by a factor of two; only the trajectory in $h$ has
        economic content. At $(\tau = 2, \gamma = 0.1)$ the bias is
        even larger because the PR is much stronger here.
\end{itemize}

\section*{What ``immortal-accurate'' actually means}
The anchor was produced by the consistent-kernel joint-limit operator
plus high precision at six grids. Two independent confirmations exist
in the repo:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{k3\_hfree\_morse/} ($G = 9$, no-kernel co-area):
        $\|F\| = 8 \cdot 10^{-15}$, deficit $\approx 0.17$ (lower
        because that experiment is at $(\gamma = 1, \tau = 1)$, a
        different cell --- but the operator is independent).
  \item \texttt{k3\_strict\_h0\_exact/} (flint marching-squares,
        $\sim 130$-digit): the strict-$h{=}0$ operator at
        $(\tau=2,\gamma=0.1)$ \emph{stalled} at $F = 0.995$ (no
        improvement from cold start) --- precisely the
        $C^0$-not-$C^1$ obstruction proven in Finding 9. Confirms the
        anchor is not reachable by strict alone, only by the
        joint-limit path.
\end{itemize}
The anchor is therefore not flint-level digits, but it is
\emph{operator-consistent} at six independent grids and confirmed by
two other operator families. That is the strongest truth-closeness
the project currently owns.

\section*{Implication for any future $\gamma$-ladder}
\textbf{A single-$h$ ladder is not adequate at any $\tau$ where the
equilibrium is more than mildly partial.} The valid product is the
$h$-trajectory plus the strict bound, as the $v2$/$v3$ design enforces.
The current dual-DD $v3$ run at $(\tau = 0.1)$ is right-shaped for that
regime (slope wedge $O(\tau^2/\gamma)$ is small, so single-cell DD
nails carry useful information) --- but the deficit it reports is the
same ``operator-conditional'' object: the only economically defensible
deliverable remains the $h$-trend toward the strict reference.
