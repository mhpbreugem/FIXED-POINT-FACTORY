\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The Chebyshev-tab prototype's $\sim 1.7\!\times\!10^{-3}$ residual floor
was diagnosed as structural at $N{=}7$ (\texttt{fp\_analysis.pdf}).
Running the user-suggested "legacy + table" test
(\texttt{test\_replay\_and\_table.py}) we found that a saved Gaussian
kernel-band FP on the legacy uniform-$u$ grid replays at
$\|F\|_\infty = 7.2\!\times\!10^{-16}$ in one step, and adding the
lookup table on top reaches $6.4\!\times\!10^{-15}$ under
Newton--Krylov. \emph{The floor was the chebroots/bisect root-extraction,
not the table.} This note replaces that step in the Chebyshev pipeline
with a Gaussian kernel band and shows the operator nails to machine
$\varepsilon$ from \emph{both} cold and warm starts at bandwidths
$h \in [0.20, 0.50]$ on the same $N{=}7$ grid. The FP shape (slope,
deficit) is the standard $h$-regularized REE: as $h \to 0$ the slope
approaches the chebroots reference at $\approx 0.34$ and the deficit
falls below $0.21$.
\end{abstract}

\section{What changed: contour extraction $\to$ Gaussian band}

Old Phase A (build $\mu(p, u_k)$ table, \texttt{cheby\_numba\_tab.py}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For each Lobatto $u_k$ and each $p$ in $p$-grid,
  \item extract the level set $\{(u_a, u_b) : P(u_a, u_b, u_k) = p\}$
        by bisecting the 1D Chebyshev polynomial in $u_b$ at each GL
        node $\xi_a^{(q)}$;
  \item integrate $f_v(u_a)\,f_v(u_b)\,/\,|\partial P/\partial u_b|$
        along the curve.
\end{enumerate}
The non-smoothness comes from step (2): roots can be born or die at the
box edge as $p$ varies, producing $C^0$ creases in $A_v(p, u_k)$.

New Phase A (kernel band, \texttt{cheby\_numba\_kern\_tab.py}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For each Lobatto $u_k$ and each $p$ in $p$-grid,
  \item evaluate $P$ on the tensor-GL grid $(\xi_a^{(q_a)},
        \xi_b^{(q_b)})$ via Cheb-3D contraction,
  \item integrate
        \[
          A_v(p, u_k) \;=\; \int\!\!\int K_h\big(P(u_a, u_b, u_k)-p\big)\,
                              f_v(u_a)\,f_v(u_b)\;du_a\,du_b,
          \quad K_h(z) = \exp\!\big(-z^2/(2h^2)\big),
        \]
        over the full 2D slice using tensor GL quadrature.
\end{enumerate}
There is no root finding. $A_v$ is an analytic function of the Cheb
coefficients of $P$ (a sum of Gaussians of polynomial differences), so
$\Phi$ is analytic in $P$ and Newton converges quadratically.

Phase B (per-cube-cell clearing) is unchanged.

\section{Result: machine $\varepsilon$ from both starts}

Both cold ($P_0 = \sigma(0.5\,T)$) and warm
($P_0 = $ \texttt{P\_final\_sym2.npy}, an old prototype FP) starts
reach machine $\varepsilon$ in $\sim 20$--$40$ Anderson iterations at
$h \in \{0.20, 0.30, 0.50\}$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rcc}
\toprule
$h$ & cold floor & warm floor \\
\midrule
0.50 & $2.2\!\times\!10^{-16}$ & $7.2\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\
0.30 & $6.7\!\times\!10^{-16}$ & $1.0\!\times\!10^{-15}$ \\
0.20 & $6.7\!\times\!10^{-16}$ & $8.9\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\
0.15 & $2.1\!\times\!10^{-4}$ \emph{(fails)} & $1.6\!\times\!10^{-4}$ \emph{(fails)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $h{=}0.15$ case is informative: at $G{=}7$ the Lobatto spacing in
$p$ near $p{=}\tfrac{1}{2}$ is $\sim 0.15$, so a band of width $0.15$
covers about one grid cell and the Gaussian kernel approaches a delta
function --- the operator approaches the discontinuous strict-$h{=}0$
limit and Anderson stalls at the same $\sim 10^{-4}$ floor we know from
\texttt{fp\_analysis.pdf}. The rule of thumb is: $h$ must span several
typical $P$-jumps between adjacent cells.

\section{What FP did we find?}

The kernel-band operator does \emph{not} have the same FP as the strict
contour operator --- $h>0$ regularizes the integral and the FP depends
on $h$. As $h \to 0$ the kernel FP converges to the strict-contour FP.

\midrule
0.50 & $0.125$ & $0.308$ \\
0.40 & $0.130$ & $0.300$ \\
0.30 & $0.142$ & $0.279$ \\

The slope rises from $0.125$ at $h{=}0.5$ toward $0.34$ (the
chebroots estimate from \texttt{tab\_explain.pdf}, $\gamma{=}1$) as $h$
narrows, and the information deficit falls from $0.31$ to $0.21$ over
the same range. The natural continuation here is a "joint $h{\to}0$
limit": refine the grid and lower $h$ together, keeping
$h \gtrsim \mathrm{const}\!\cdot\!\Delta u$, until the FP stabilises ---
the same recipe the legacy \texttt{phi\_K3\_halo\_smooth} solver uses
in \texttt{projects/REZN}.

\section{Why this works: $\Phi$ is now analytic in $P$}

The new $A_v(p, u_k)$ is a sum of terms
$K_h(P(\xi^{(q_a)}, \xi^{(q_b)}, \xi_{\text{fix}}) - p)
 \cdot f_v(u_a^{(q_a)}) f_v(u_b^{(q_b)}) \cdot w$,
each of which is an analytic function of the Cheb coefficients of $P$.
Hence the Jacobian $\partial F / \partial P$ is well-defined and smooth
everywhere, Newton's quadratic convergence applies, and Anderson is
similarly unencumbered. The previous operator had $A_v$ defined as a
piecewise function with $C^0$ creases at root-birth events; Newton's
finite-difference Jacobian was being computed across those creases.

\section{Implementation}

\texttt{/tmp/cheby\_h0/cheby\_numba\_kern\_tab.py} ($\sim 150$ LOC,
all numba-jit'd). Hot path:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{cheb\_eval\_slice\_axis0} --- evaluate $P$ on the
        $N_{Q}{\times}N_Q$ tensor-GL grid for one Lobatto $u_k$
        ($G \cdot N_Q^2 \cdot G^2$ ops),
  \item \texttt{build\_mu\_table\_kern} --- inner $(p, q_a, q_b)$ loop
        computes $A_v$ from the slice with a precomputed Gaussian
        weight ($G_p \cdot N_Q^2$ ops per $u_k$, $\cdot G$ for the
        full table),
  \item \texttt{phi\_kern\_tab\_jit} --- standard per-cube-cell lookup
        + \texttt{crra\_clear\_jit}.
\end{enumerate}

Per-$\Phi$ wall time at $N{=}6$, $G_p{=}121$: $\sim 7$\,ms (vs
$\sim 9$\,ms for the chebroots-tab variant). Newton--Krylov nail from
cold: $\sim 0.2$\,s wall time.

\section{Verdict}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The lookup-table architecture (\texttt{tab\_explain.pdf}) is
        \emph{not} the source of the $10^{-3}$ floor. The smooth-band
        operator with the same table reaches machine $\varepsilon$.
  \item Replacing chebroots/bisect with a Gaussian kernel band of
        bandwidth $h \gtrsim 2\!-\!3\,\Delta u$ gives an analytic
        $\Phi$ that Newton can nail; cold and warm starts both work.
  \item The price is that the FP depends on $h$. The recipe is the
        standard kernel-band joint limit: refine grid and $h$
        together.
\end{itemize}
