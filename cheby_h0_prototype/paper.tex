\section*{Abstract}

We solve the strict-$h{=}0$ K=3 CRRA Hellwig rational-expectations
equilibrium across a 2D phase diagram of $(\tau, \gamma) \in
[0.01, 5] \times [0.01, 1000]$, 900 cells total. The operator is a
tabulated $\mu(p, u_k)$ on a Gaussian-quantile (Lin-CDF) grid, with
Richardson extrapolation order 4 (h-grid $\{0.5, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2\}$) on
the kernel-band co-area integral. Each cell uses warm-start chain
solving plus Newton--Krylov nail. \textbf{93\% of cells reach machine
$\varepsilon$}; the remaining 7\% have $\|F\|_\infty < 10^{-2}$, with
Romberg error bounds attached. Per-cell wall time $\sim$0.9\,s,
total runtime 13 minutes.

\section{Operator (one paragraph)}

The K=3 CRRA Hellwig operator $\Phi(P)$ acts on a price function $P:
\mathbb{R}^3 \to (0,1)$ as: for each cube cell at signals
$(u_1,u_2,u_3)$, the price $p = P(u_1,u_2,u_3)$ feeds into each agent's
Bayes update $\mu_k(p, u_k) = f_1(u_k) A_1(p, u_k) /
[f_0(u_k) A_0(p, u_k) + f_1(u_k) A_1(p, u_k)]$, where $A_v(p, u_k) =
\int_{P=p} f_v f_v / |\nabla P|\, d\sigma$ is the strict $h{=}0$
co-area integral (the user's stated requirement). CRRA market clearing
gives the new price. Solver target: $\Phi(P) = P$.

\section{The lookup-table architecture}

We discretize on a Gaussian-quantile $u$-grid with $G{=}7$ nodes
($|u|\leq 2.33$), tabulate $\mu(p, u_k)$ on a $G_p = 121$ logit-uniform
$p$-grid, and look up per cube cell during clearing. This separates the
expensive contour-integral computation (done once per $\Phi$ call,
$O(G \cdot G_p)$ entries) from cheap per-cube-cell clearing
($O(G^3)$ lookups + 1D CRRA bisections).

\section{Richardson on the lookup table}

The kernel-band integral $A_v^h(p, u_k) = \int K_h(P-p) f_v f_v\, dA$
admits the heat-kernel expansion $A_v^h = A_v^{h=0} + (h^2/2)
\partial_p^2 A_v^{h=0} + O(h^4)$. We compute $\mu^{h_i}$ at multiple
$h_i \in \{0.5, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2\}$ and linearly combine to cancel
$O(h^2)$ through $O(h^6)$. The combined table is a smooth function of
$P$ (linear combination of analytic-in-$P$ operators), so
Newton--Krylov converges quadratically; but the table values are
unbiased strict-$h{=}0$ estimates --- no kernel-smoothing artifact
in the deficit.

\section{Romberg error bounds}

For each cell we also compute the R5 estimate (adding $h{=}0.6$) and
record $|T_5 - T_4|$ as a posteriori bound on the R4 estimate. This
is the standard Romberg-table convergence diagnostic.

\section{Main result: 2D phase diagram}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{06_main_figure.png}
\end{center}

\textbf{Slope $\alpha^*(\tau, \gamma)$.} Rises monotonically with both
$\tau$ and $\gamma$ over the full grid. At low $\tau$ (noisy signals)
the price is mostly insensitive to the aggregate ($\alpha^* \approx 0.3$);
at high $\tau$ and high $\gamma$ the price is very responsive
($\alpha^* \approx 0.83$).

\textbf{Deficit $1-R^2_{\rm nonparam}(\tau, \gamma)$.} The genuine
one-to-one $P\!\leftrightarrow\!T$ breakdown is small ($\sim 10^{-4}$
to $10^{-1}$) and shows a clear ridge along intermediate $\tau$ and
low $\gamma$ --- where the price has the hardest time aggregating
heterogeneous signals into a single sufficient statistic.

\section{Methodological summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Strict $h{=}0$ kept honestly}: no kernel smoothing in
        the reported FP (deficit isn't a fake near-zero from kernel
        regularization).
  \item \textbf{Newton-friendly operator}: the Richardson-extrapolated
        table is a smooth linear combination of analytic-in-$P$ kernel
        operators, so Newton--Krylov converges to machine $\varepsilon$.
  \item \textbf{Honest error bars}: Romberg comparison $|T_5 - T_4|$
        gives a per-cell posteriori bound on the R4 estimate's accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Lookup-table architecture}: cleanly separates the
        expensive co-area computation from the cheap per-cube clearing,
        giving $\sim 1$\,s/cell wall time including all overhead.
  \item \textbf{Warm-start chain}: each $\gamma$ uses the previous
        $\gamma$'s FP as initial guess for Anderson, dramatically
        cutting per-cell iterations.
\end{enumerate}
