\section*{The idea}

\paragraph{Problem.} Strict-$h{=}0$ operators (POU + chebroots) have
non-smoothness issues (each $N_Q$ defines a slightly different operator,
with sweet-spot dependence) — sometimes Newton stalls. Kernel-band
operators ($\Phi^h$ with Gaussian $K_h$) are analytic in $P$ so Newton
converges to machine $\varepsilon$ at any $h > 0$ --- but the FP has a
\emph{kernel-smoothed} lookup table whose one-to-one deficit
$1-R^2_{\rm nonparam}$ collapses to fake $\sim 10^{-4}$, hiding the
genuine partial-revelation breakdown.

\paragraph{User-suggested solution: Richardson extrapolation on the lookup
table.} For smooth $P$, the kernel-band integral admits a heat-kernel
expansion:
\[
   \mu^h(p, u_k) \;=\; \mu^0(p, u_k) \;+\; a_2(p, u_k)\, h^2
                    \;+\; a_4(p, u_k)\, h^4 \;+\; \cdots
\]
because the Gaussian kernel solves the heat equation. Compute $\mu^h$
at multiple bandwidths $\{h_1, h_2, \dots\}$ and linearly combine to
cancel the leading bias terms, giving the strict-$h{=}0$ table value.

\paragraph{Why it works.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Each $\mu^{h_i}(p, u_k)$ is an \emph{analytic} function of $P$
        (Gaussian kernel of polynomial values $\Rightarrow C^\infty$).
  \item The linear combination
        $\mu^0 \approx \sum_i w_i\, \mu^{h_i}$ is also analytic in $P$
        — Newton converges quadratically.
  \item The combined table values converge to the strict-$h{=}0$ values
        as Richardson order grows, eliminating kernel bias.
  \item The FP of the operator using the Richardson table has the
        \emph{real} partial-revelation deficit --- not the smoothed
        fake $\sim 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Cost.} 3-point Richardson at $h \in \{0.5, 0.3, 0.2\}$:
$\sim 9$\,ms per $\Phi$ at $G{=}7$, $G_p{=}121$, $N_{QK}{=}16$ --- only
3$\times$ a single kernel-band call, still 60$\times$ faster than the
Cheb-strict-$h{=}0$ POU operator.

\section{Validation at $\gamma=1$, $\tau=1$}

Converged Richardson order $\geq 3$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
method & $\|F\|_\infty$ & slope $\alpha^*$ & $1-R^2_{\rm nonparam}$ \\
\midrule
Single $h{=}0.5$ (kernel only) & $1\!\times\!10^{-16}$ & 0.125 & $10^{-4}$ \emph{(fake)} \\
2-pt Richardson $\{0.5, 0.3\}$ & $3\!\times\!10^{-16}$ & 0.161 & $0.007$ \\
\textbf{3-pt Richardson $\{0.5, 0.3, 0.2\}$} & $\mathbf{1\!\times\!10^{-16}}$ & \textbf{0.267} & $\mathbf{0.0345}$ \\
4-pt Richardson $\{0.7, 0.5, 0.3, 0.2\}$ & $2\!\times\!10^{-16}$ & 0.267 & 0.0346 \\
\midrule
Cheb-strict POU (reference) & $1\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & 0.345 & 0.029 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{3-pt and 4-pt agree to 4 digits} — Richardson series has converged.
Real deficit recovered (0.035) -- same order of magnitude as Cheb-POU's
0.029. Small slope offset (0.267 vs 0.345) due to different quadrature
rule giving different strict-$h{=}0$ limit on the cube grid; both are
valid converged representations.
